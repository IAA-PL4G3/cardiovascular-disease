Why Recall Matters

In cardiovascular risk prediction, a missed positive is more dangerous than a false alarm.

The cost of a false negative

Recall (sensitivity) measures how many actual high-risk patients the model correctly flags. A low-recall model misses sick patients - they leave without intervention and may suffer a cardiac event. A false positive, by contrast, leads to an unnecessary follow-up: annoying, but not fatal. For a decision-support tool in a clinical setting, maximising recall at an acceptable precision is the right trade-off.

Threshold analysis

The default threshold of 0.5 is not optimal for recall. Lowering it to 0.4 increases recall significantly at the cost of some precision - more patients flagged, fewer missed.

Selective prediction

By only predicting on cases where the model is confident, accuracy climbs above 85% - deferring uncertain cases to a clinician rather than forcing a low-confidence call.

Baseline vs Feature Engineering

Impact of replacing raw age, height and weight with BMI and age in years.

What changed

The baseline uses raw features: age in days, height (cm), weight (kg). Feature engineering replaces these with BMI (weight / height²) and age in years - more clinically meaningful and less correlated with each other. The scaler and KNN imputer used at inference are trained on the engineered feature space.
